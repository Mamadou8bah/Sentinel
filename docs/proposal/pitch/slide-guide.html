Sutura and Sentinel

Slide-by-slide guide. Sixteen slides. Read the explanation before the pitch. The green box is what to say out loud. The blue box is for questions.

Sutura is the wallet. A customer, an agent, a shop, and an admin use it. They can send money, cash in, cash out, and collect with a payment link. Every payment is written as two matching records. The balance on the screen is the sum of those records.

Sentinel is the check. The customer never opens Sentinel and never has a Sentinel account. Sutura calls Sentinel when a wallet is opened, and again before each payment. Sentinel says allow, review, or block, and it writes the reason. Staff work the review cases. They cannot change the balance.

The order of the pitch is: a real case, the wallets people already use, the two problems, then the project itself. Slides 5 to 13 cover the wallet and Sentinel. Slide 14 closes.

Wave is the wallet most people use. On Wave, the ID can be uploaded later. On Afrimoney, signup takes an ID number. Do not say either of them checks the person at signup. Sutura does: the customer shows the ID, and Sentinel checks that a live person is there, before the wallet pays.

This is a student project for examination. It is not a Central Bank licence, and the fraud score is not trained on live Wave or Afrimoney payments. The Kanifing matter is a charge. The plea was not guilty.

Slide 3 · The problem

Two things go wrong.

1. Signup does not check the person. On Wave you can upload your ID later. On Afrimoney you type an ID number. The wallet can still pay. Change the SIM and the PIN, and the money can move. That is the Kanifing charge.

2. The balance is only a number. If a payment is sent twice, or stops halfway, that number can be wrong. There is no list of records to show what the balance should be.

Card 2, the one number

Many wallet builds store one balance and add or subtract. Three failures follow.

  • The phone retries a cash-in after a timeout. If the first request succeeded, the second one adds the same money again.
  • A transfer updates one wallet and then fails before the other wallet is updated. The two balances no longer match.
  • Someone asks which payments produced the figure on the screen. The old number has been overwritten, so there is no list to rebuild from.

You do not have Wave’s or Afrimoney’s internal books. If they ask, say that. Card 2 is the failure of a wallet that keeps only one number. Sutura is built so those three failures are refused in tests.

Slide 5 · Sutura

What the wallet does.

A customer opens a wallet, sends money, and receives money. An agent takes cash in, or pays cash out. A shop collects with a payment link. An admin can freeze a wallet, or undo a payment. Each action is saved as two matching records. The balance is those records added up.

The four users

Customer. Opens a Sutura wallet, sees a balance, sends money to another Sutura wallet, and receives money. The balance on the phone is the sum of the records. The phone does not keep a private number that counts as the truth.

Agent. This is the cash in and cash out the quarterly volumes are about. The customer asks for cash in or cash out. The agent looks up that request and marks the cash as handed over. Only then does Sutura write the two records. An agent cannot undo a customer’s wallet and cannot open the Sentinel staff screen.

Shop. A merchant registers, holds a collections wallet, and can issue a payment link. A customer pays that link the same way they send money: two matching records, after Sentinel has answered.

Admin. Can freeze a wallet so it cannot pay, and can undo a payment. An undo is itself a new pair of records. It is not an edit of the old number. Admin also sees a reconciliation report: if the sum of the records and the payment history disagree, the mismatch is written down.

There is also a developer key, so another server can call Sutura. You can skip that unless they ask about integration.

Say: Four people use the Sutura wallet. The customer sends and receives. The agent handles cash in and cash out. A shop collects with a payment link. An admin can freeze a wallet or undo a payment. Every one of those actions is saved as two matching records, and the balance is the sum.

If they ask what “undo” means: a reversal is a new payment written in the opposite direction, with its own record. The original lines stay in the list, so you can still see what happened.

Slide 6 · Sutura

You cash in D1,000. The wallet writes it twice.

The cash box goes up by D1,000. Your wallet goes up by D1,000. If the two amounts do not match, the payment is refused. If the same payment is sent again, it is refused. The number on the screen is the sum of these records.

Why it is written twice

Cash-in is the customer handing notes to an agent and receiving wallet value. Both sides have to be saved, or the books can lie. The cash box is the dalasi the platform is holding. The wallet is what the platform owes that customer. D1,000 in must show up as D1,000 owed to the customer. If one side were D1,000 and the other side were D900, Sutura refuses the whole payment and saves nothing.

In the proposal this is double-entry. You do not need the term unless they ask. “Written twice, and the two sides must match” is the idea. If they ask: the wallet account is a liability, because the platform owes the customer. The cash box is an asset. A liability balance is credits minus debits. An asset balance is debits minus credits. The deposit debits platform cash and credits the wallet, under one shared id called a trace.

The repeat

If the agent’s phone times out and sends the same cash-in again, the second copy is refused. The trace, or the idempotency key on the request, already exists. The customer is credited once. That is the answer to “the balance is only a number”: a retry cannot add the money twice, because the second write is rejected.

The screen

Nothing on the phone is allowed to be a separate balance. After the records are saved, the balance shown is the sum of that customer’s records. If the posting fails, the payment is marked failed, and the balance does not change.

Say: Cash in one thousand dalasi. Sutura writes the cash box up by one thousand, and the wallet up by one thousand. Those two lines are one payment. If the amounts do not match, it is refused and nothing is saved. If the same cash-in is sent again, the copy is refused. The number on the screen is the sum of the records.

If the write fails, the payment fails and neither balance changes.

Slide 8 · Sentinel

Three questions before the wallet pays.

Is this the real person? You show the ID, and Sentinel checks that a live person is there. A weak result stops the account. Wave and Afrimoney do not do this when you sign up.

Is the paper real? The file is checked for changes. A signature is compared only when we already have a sample. No sample means that check is skipped.

Is this payment safe? Allow, review, or block. The reason is written down. A staff member must add a note before the case is closed.

The person

Sutura opens a check and sends the customer to a hosted page. She captures the identity document and completes a liveness check, meaning a live person is in front of the camera, not a printed photo. Weak face match or weak liveness comes back flagged or rejected. Higher limits on the wallet can wait for a verified result. This is the difference from the apps people use now. Wave lets you upload the ID later. Afrimoney asks for an ID number. Neither of those is a document plus a live check at signup.

The paper

Cheque or invoice images are checked for tampering and for fields that disagree. A signature comparison needs a sample enrolled earlier. If there is no sample, that one check is skipped and the other checks still run. A missing sample is not treated as a match. Say “skipped,” not “matched.”

The payment

Sutura sends the amount, the time, the channel, and who is on the other side. Rules and a model return a score, the flags that fired, a short explanation, and allow, review, or block. The explanation attributes the score to the inputs, using a method such as SHAP, so a reviewer can see which signals mattered. You can say “the reason names the signals” unless they ask for the method name.

The model is trained and evaluated on published or synthetic data: PaySim-style payment traces, signature sets such as CEDAR, and synthetic identity and cheque images. It is not trained on live Wave or Afrimoney transactions. If a model is not wired yet, the API returns a fixed rule-based result and records that a model did not run. Say that if they ask whether the score is real.

Say: Before the wallet pays, Sentinel asks three things. Is this the real person? You show the ID, and it checks that you are there. A weak result stops the account. Wave lets the ID wait. Afrimoney takes an ID number. Second, is the document real? A signature is scored only if we already have a sample. Third, is the payment safe? Allow, review, or block, and the reason is written down. Staff must add a note.

Slide 9 · Sentinel

Three answers. The money follows.

Allow. The wallet pays. Sutura writes the two records, and the balance changes.

Review. The money waits. Staff read the reason and write a note. Then Sutura pays or stops.

Block. The wallet does not pay. No record is saved. If Sentinel cannot be reached, the wallet also does not pay.

The order

The check happens before the records are saved. Allow is the only answer that lets Sutura write the payment. Review leaves the payment pending and opens or links a case. Block rejects the movement. There is no half-written balance on any of the three paths.

When staff later approve or reject a review, Sentinel tells Sutura. Sutura then completes the payment, fails it, or keeps it waiting. The write still happens only inside Sutura, and only once.

If Sentinel cannot be reached, silence is not treated as allow. The payment stays pending or fails, and the attempt is logged. That is fail closed: when the check cannot answer, the wallet withholds the payment.

Say: The money follows the answer. Allow means Sutura writes the two records and the balance changes. Review means nothing is written yet. Staff read the reason, write a note, and then the wallet pays or stops. Block means no record is saved. If Sentinel is down, the wallet also does not pay.

Slide 11 · Two screens

The customer stays in the wallet.

Sutura. The customer, the agent, the shop, and the admin. Sending, cash in, cash out, and payment links all happen here. The balance changes only here.

Sentinel. Staff only. They see the case, the reason for allow, review, or block, and they must write a note. They cannot pay, and they cannot change the balance. The customer has no Sentinel account.

Sutura asks Sentinel when the wallet is opened, and again before each payment.

Who logs in where

Sutura roles are customer, merchant, agent, admin, and developer. Each role can call only its own actions. An agent completes a cash reference. An agent cannot reverse a wallet. A customer cannot open Sentinel.

Sentinel staff roles are admin, compliance, and analyst. They log in to a desk: overview, cases, customers, tools, analytics, settings, and an audit list. Long lists are paginated. There is no signup for the person who holds the wallet.

The two calls are fixed in the project, not added at the end. At onboarding, Sutura creates a Sentinel session and stores the status that comes back. Before a transfer, a cash-in settlement, or a cash-out is completed, Sutura asks for a score. The callback from Sentinel is signed, so a forged message cannot complete a cash-in or a KYC result.

Slide 12 · The staff screen

What staff do with a case.

A payment on review opens a case. They see the amount, the person, and the reason for the score. They decide, and they must write a note. A decision with no note is refused. The screen keeps who decided, and when. Sutura then pays, stops, or keeps waiting. Staff never type a new balance.

What the case contains

The case is opened when the score, or a rule, says the payment should not pass quietly. Staff see the amount, the channel, the other party, and the short explanation: which signals drove review or block. They choose a decision. The note is mandatory. Saving a decision with an empty note is rejected by the system. The audit row stores who acted and when. That is the trail behind a quarterly fraud report: not only a score, but a named person and a written reason.

After they decide, Sentinel notifies Sutura. Sutura completes the pending payment, fails it, or leaves it waiting. Staff do not edit the wallet balance. If the decision is to pay, Sutura writes the two matching records itself, once.

Say: When a payment is on review, it opens a case. Staff see the amount, the person, and why the score said review. They decide, and they have to write a note. No note, no decision. The screen stores who decided and when. Sutura then pays or stops. Staff never type a new balance.

If they ask where the reason comes from: rules plus a model. The explanation names the features that produced the score. The model is evaluated on published simulated payments, because there is no public file of live Gambian wallet transactions you are allowed to train on.

Slide 13 · How I will know

I will try to break both sides.

A payment whose two sides do not match is refused. The same payment sent twice is saved once. An agent cannot undo a customer’s wallet. A customer cannot open the staff screen. A weak ID check does not pass. No signature sample means that check is skipped. One full run: open a wallet, cash in, allow one payment, hold another, and show the records and the case.

What each test is proving

The two sides. An unequal payment must be rejected with no rows saved. A reconciliation run on a correct history reports no mismatch. A history broken on purpose reports one mismatch, so the report can see an error.

The repeat. One transfer, sent again with the same key, moves the money once. That is the test for the timeout problem on card 2 of the problem slide.

The roles. An agent token that tries to undo a wallet gets an authorisation failure. A customer token does not open the Sentinel desk. The two logins stay separate.

The ID and the signature. A weak liveness sample comes back flagged or rejected, not verified. A document with no enrolled signature sample does not come back as a signature match.

The score. On a held-out slice of PaySim-style data, the report includes precision, recall, and the false-alarm rate. A sample of explanations is read by hand to check that the named features are the ones that drove the review. Dataset shift is a written limit: simulated payments are not live Gambian transactions.

The full run. Register, pass or fail the ID check, cash in, one payment Sentinel allows, one payment Sentinel reviews, then show the records and the case. That script is the demonstration.

The questions the build has to answer

  1. After a cash-in is retried, reversed, or disputed, can the balance be rebuilt from the records?
  2. Which steps wait for allow, review, or block: opening the wallet, cashing in, and sending money?
  3. Can Sentinel give a reason, while the money stays in the Sutura wallet?

The method is design science: state the problem, build the wallet and the check against those rules, and evaluate them with the tests above. The written report includes the failures. There is no live connection to the national switch, and there is no Central Bank licence in this project.
